# Unprompted optimism

I've started making art again.

It started with [Unprompted](https://unprompted.clothing), and making clothes. That bled into interior product designs, buying a 3D printer, and now, finally, plans for some physical, floating canvas-style prints.

Quite a few different outputs. But they all seem to use the same bit of my brain.

When something doesn't work artistically, I find it quite easy to problem solve. The proportions are wrong? Change them. The material doesn't behave how I expected? Try another one. The thing in my head can't exist in the real world yet? That's kinda the fun bit.

I assume there is a way through. I'm curious. Weirdly optimistic, even.

This is not how my brain reacts to business anymore.

After Magpai, and everything I went through on the business side, uncertainty still closes my mind. A problem quickly becomes binary: it works or it doesn't. It was a good decision or a terrible one. The future is exciting or everything is completely fucked.

Usually my brain speed-runs to the second option.

Which is strange, because building a business is obviously also a creative problem-solving exercise. Or at least, it should be.

Perhaps the difference is that a bad 3D print is allowed to just be a bad 3D print. It doesn't say anything about me, my future, the last few years, or whether I made the right decisions. I can throw it in a box and print another one.

Business problems still feel heavier than the problem in front of me. They arrive carrying all the other problems with them.

I don't have a neat answer for that yet. But making things again feels like a useful place to start.

\- Ben
