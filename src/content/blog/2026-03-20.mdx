# The new .vimrc

Right now there is an explosion of devs building AI agent orchestrators. My Twitter timeline is full of them. And hey, guilty, [I made one too](https://get-hoo.com).

![Hoo](https://www.get-hoo.com/hero.png)

I think what's happening here is actually quite familiar.

Developers have always tweaked their terminal. You pick your favorite emulator, customize the colors, the fonts, your vim config, your emacs shortcuts, your shell aliases. You spend more time in your dotfiles than you'd ever admit.

In a world where software can be fully prompted into existence, it feels like developers are going to build - or fork - their orchestration tools. Same energy. Your orchestrator is your new .vimrc.

---

What's interesting is how similar most of them look.

Nearly all have a vertical sidebar of project-level groups, and then horizontal tabs for each project's sessions. Some have the ability to split the screen in two.

---

A lot of people are just going to be happy with tmux.

![tmux](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/Demo-tmux.png)

Tmux + Claude Code in a few panes is already a pretty compelling setup. It's battle-tested by decades of terminal nerds, and it works. If you're not the type to fuss over your tools (good for you), tmux will probably do the job just fine.

---

My own experience building one? Super fun. It's a nice outlet and a great way to explore how I actually want to work with AI agents. What do I reach for? What annoys me? Where do I want more control?

But it doesn't feel like something you should raise money for. Or even sell. It's too personal, too opinionated - too much a reflection of one person's workflow. (I've open sourced my one, feel free to fork and jam on it).

---

One thing I have been thinking about is taking this a step further - spatially remembering where different things are. Not just tabs and panes, but a genuine sense of *place* for your projects and agents.
